# Audience, Reach and Frequency

## Reach
Reach is the number of unique households that saw an ad at least once. Connected TV reach is measured at the household level, not the person level, because several people often watch one screen together.

## Frequency
Frequency is impressions divided by households reached. It tells you how many times, on average, each household saw the ad during the flight.

## Healthy frequency ranges
For most brand campaigns a frequency between 3 and 5 over a four-week flight is healthy. Above 6, households start to see the same ad so often that recall stops improving and complaints about repetition rise. Below 2, many households will not remember the ad at all.

## Frequency caps
A frequency cap limits how many times one household can see an ad in a day or a week. Caps are set per publisher, so a household watching several apps can exceed the intended total. Cross-publisher caps are only possible when the campaign is bought through a single platform that can recognise the same household across apps.

## Reading reach with spend
When two campaigns spend similar amounts, the one with higher reach and lower frequency is usually spreading budget more efficiently. When reach is flat but spend keeps rising, the campaign is buying repeat views of the same homes.
