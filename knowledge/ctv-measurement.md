# CTV Measurement Basics

## What counts as a view
An impression is counted when the first frame of the ad is rendered on a connected TV screen. A completed view is counted when the final quartile beacon fires, which means the viewer reached the last frame of the ad without skipping or closing the app.

## Completion rate
Completion rate is completed views divided by impressions. On connected TV, most ads cannot be skipped, so completion rates above 85 percent are normal. Rates under 75 percent usually point to one of three causes: the ad is running in apps that allow the viewer to exit easily, the ad is longer than the content break, or the ad is being served to devices where the TV is switched off but the streaming box is still on.

## Quartiles
Most players report four checkpoints: 25, 50, 75 and 100 percent. A sharp drop between 25 and 50 percent usually means the opening seconds of the creative are not holding attention.

## Average seconds watched
Average seconds watched is useful when ads of different lengths are compared. A 15-second ad with 13 seconds watched on average is performing better than a 30-second ad with 21 seconds watched, even though the raw number is lower.

## Measurement partners
Independent verification partners sample a share of impressions and may report slightly different numbers from the publisher. Differences of up to five percent are expected and should not be treated as errors.
